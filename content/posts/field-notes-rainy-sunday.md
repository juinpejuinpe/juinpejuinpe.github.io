The rain started before dawn and has not once apologized. It taps on the
window with the patience of someone who plans to stay, and the whole city
seems to agree: this is not a day for running errands.

## Small observations

From my window I can see the street the way you see a friend who is thinking
— the surface calm, the real activity underneath. Cars pass slowly, their
wipers keeping time. A woman with a yellow umbrella walks against the wind
and wins, barely.

The bakery on the corner has turned its sign on early. Through the steamed
glass I imagine the first trays coming out: croissants, still shivering from
the oven. I will go down later, I tell myself. Not yet.

## The particular quiet

Rainy Sundays have their own acoustics. The usual noises — construction,
motorcycles, arguments conducted at full volume — are muffled. Even my own
apartment sounds different: the kettle is louder, the pages of my book turn
like small doors closing.

There is a freedom in this kind of day. The world has excused us from
productivity. Nothing is expected, so everything is possible: a long bath, a
bad movie, a chapter written in a single sitting.

## What the rain leaves behind

By evening the rain will stop, as rain always does. The streets will shine
like oiled leather, and the air will carry the smell of wet earth and
possibility. We will forget, until the next storm, how good it feels to be
slowed down.

For now, the rain continues, and I am in no hurry. The tea is hot. The book is
good. The day is doing exactly what it was made for.
